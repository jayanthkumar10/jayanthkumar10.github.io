Minimization of False Negatives in Healthcare

The Problem

Cardiovascular disease is a leading cause of mortality globally. Building automated screening models allows clinical professionals to isolate high-risk indicators earlier. However, raw clinical datasets present massive challenges, including missing values (imputed values can skew diagnostics), statistical noise, and high correlations between patient variables.

In clinical machine learning, optimizing pure **accuracy** is dangerous. A false negative (missing a patient with heart disease) has lethal consequences, meaning the model's **recall** rate must be prioritized, requiring highly precise threshold tuning and cross-validation pipelines.

Core Engineering Decisions

LOCF Imputation vs Global Mean Replacement

The Alternative

Fill missing patient parameters with the dataset's global mean or median values.

The Decision

Using global statistical averages masks crucial individual patient variations and creates artificial correlations. I implemented Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) for sequential patient records, ensuring that missing attributes are filled using the patient's own historical observations to preserve context.

Multi-Model Framework vs Monolithic Tuning

The Alternative

Select a standard classifier (like Logistic Regression) immediately and optimize it exclusively.

The Decision

I built a comparative evaluation framework that pipelines patient features to Logistic Regression, SVM, KNN, Naive Bayes, Decision Trees, and Random Forest. Evaluating them simultaneously demonstrated that Random Forest yielded the highest stability and recall performance.

5-Fold Cross Validation over Single Splits

The Alternative

Rely on a simple 80/20 train-test split to report final metric evaluations.

The Decision

Single splits are susceptible to the "luck of the draw", leading to over-optimistic test scores. Running a robust 5-Fold Cross Validation splits the data into five independent rotations, guaranteeing that the final precision and recall scores represent stable, generalizable performance.

Feature Selection via Classifier Importance

The Alternative

Train models on all raw patient features without checking for statistical variance.

The Decision

Clinical datasets contain noisy variables that lead to overfitting. I utilized Random Forest feature importance scores to isolate and drop low-influence attributes. This minimized model complexity, accelerated inference speeds, and improved generalization on test folds.

Trade-off: Dropping features can hide subtle multi-variable interactions, but drastically improves model robustness and prevents overfitting.

Operational Outcome

The anomaly detection project showcases a complete supervised learning pipeline. By incorporating feature selection, comparing multi-model architectures, and evaluating results using precision and recall curves, it presents a professional framework for medical diagnostic assistance.
