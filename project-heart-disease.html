Clinical Cardiac Anomaly Detection

A complete end-to-end supervised machine learning workflow focused on clinical anomaly prediction. Features exhaustive exploratory data analysis, algorithm comparative analysis, and 5-fold cross-validation.

The Problem

Heart disease is one of the leading causes of death worldwide. Early prediction can help doctors identify high-risk patients and begin preventive treatment earlier. The objective of this project was to build a binary classification model that predicts whether a patient has heart disease by learning patterns from historical clinical data.

Engineering Decisions

LOCF Imputation over Mean Imputation

The Alternative

Replace missing clinical data points with the mathematical mean or median of the entire dataset.

The Decision

I utilized Forward Fill (LOCF). In sequential or patient-level clinical data, applying a global mean can aggressively skew individual patient profiles, masking the exact anomalies the model needs to detect.

Algorithm Ensembling vs Single Models

The Alternative

Pick a single standard algorithm like Logistic Regression and tune it extensively.

The Decision

Instead of assuming one model is superior, I built a comparative evaluation pipeline. I trained and evaluated Logistic Regression, SVM, KNN, Naive Bayes, Decision Trees, and Random Forest simultaneously. Random Forest ultimately provided the best stability.

5-Fold Cross Validation

The Alternative

Rely purely on a standard 80/20 train-test split for final accuracy reporting.

The Decision

A single split can result in optimistic or pessimistic accuracy scores due to the luck of the draw. I implemented 5-Fold Cross Validation to ensure the model's performance estimates were dependable and practically generalized across all segments of unseen data.

Feature Selection via Importance

The Alternative

Train the model on all available clinical features without filtering.

The Decision

I utilized Random Forest feature importance to identify and drop low-variance, noisy columns. This reduced the dimensionality of the dataset, lowering the chance of overfitting and improving the inference speed of the final deployed model.

Trade-off: Dropping features always carries a slight risk of losing nuanced correlations, but yields a significantly more robust model in production.

The Outcome

This project demonstrates a rigorous mastery of the complete machine learning pipeline—from foundational data cleaning to objective multi-algorithm comparison and evaluation using Precision, Recall, and F1 scores rather than relying on flawed, generic Accuracy metrics.
